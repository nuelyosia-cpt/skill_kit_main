# Ideas: 2026-11-17 (b): amazing people stories

Ten fact-checked, mind-blowing true stories about how a person (or animal) became famous. These use the channel's secondary theme ("how an infamous person came to be"). Each key fact was web-searched on 2026-11-17; sources are at the bottom. Nothing graphic: disasters are shown through rescue and survival.

## 1. The Woman the Sea Couldn't Sink (Recommended)
- **Hook:** "One woman was on board for the Titanic, its sister ship's crash, and its other sister ship's sinking, and she survived all three."
- **Story:** Violet Jessop, a White Star Line stewardess. In 1911 she is on the *Olympic* when it collides with the warship HMS *Hawke*. In 1912 she is on the *Titanic*, and escapes in lifeboat 16. In 1916 she is a nurse on the hospital ship *Britannic* when it hits a mine.
- **Ending (engagement):** They called her "Miss Unsinkable", and she kept going back to sea afterwards.
- **Why it animates:** three near-identical giant ships; a "1… 2… 3…" counter; Violet calmly stepping into a lifeboat each time with the same little suitcase.
- **Fact status:** documented (crew records, her own memoir).

## 2. The Dog Who Waited Ten Years
- **Hook:** "For almost ten years, a dog went to the same train station every day to meet a man who was never coming back."
- **Story:** Hachikō meets Professor Ueno at Shibuya Station every evening. In 1925 the professor dies suddenly at work. Hachikō keeps coming to the station, every day, for nearly ten years.
- **Ending (moral):** In 1934, Tokyo put up a bronze statue of him, and Hachikō was at the unveiling. Today it's the most famous meeting spot in Tokyo. Loyalty made him a legend.
- **Why it animates:** the same station clock through four seasons, cherry blossoms, snow, crowds rushing past a still dog, then the statue.
- **Fact status:** documented (newspapers of the time, the statue). Some details, like the exact station spot, were polished in retellings.

## 3. The Fool Who Got Rich by Accident
- **Hook:** "A man wrote a whole book with no punctuation, then added a page of just punctuation and told readers to sprinkle it in themselves."
- **Story:** "Lord" Timothy Dexter of Newburyport, Massachusetts, in the late 1700s. By his own boast, people tricked him into silly trades, like shipping bed-warming pans to the hot West Indies, and he somehow made money every time.
- **Ending (twist):** His 1802 book *A Pickle for the Knowing Ones* has almost no punctuation. A later edition added a page of commas and full stops: "pepper and salt it as they please".
- **Why it animates:** ships full of absurd cargo, merchants laughing, then gold coins pouring back; a page of floating commas.
- **Fact status:** the book and its punctuation page are documented. The trades are anecdotes, mostly from his own boasting.

## 4. The Pirate Queen Who Retired Rich
- **Hook:** "The most successful pirate in history was a woman, and she retired with all her treasure."
- **Story:** Zheng Yi Sao (Ching Shih) takes over the Red Flag Fleet in the South China Sea after her husband dies in 1807. She runs it with strict written rules and beats navies sent to stop her.
- **Ending (twist):** In 1810 she negotiates her own surrender. She keeps her loot, her crews go free, and she lives on as a businesswoman.
- **Why it animates:** junks with red sails filling the sea, a rulebook scroll, a palace negotiation, her walking away with treasure chests.
- **Fact status:** documented, but fleet sizes vary a lot between sources (hundreds to about 1,500 ships), so the script says "hundreds of ships".

## 5. The Bear Who Joined the Army
- **Hook:** "During World War Two, a bear was enlisted as a soldier, with a rank, a pay book and rations."
- **Story:** In 1942, Polish soldiers in Iran adopt a bear cub named Wojtek. He drinks from bottles, wrestles soldiers and rides in trucks. At Monte Cassino in 1944, the story goes, he helps carry crates of artillery shells.
- **Ending (engagement):** A bear carrying a shell became his company's official badge. He spent his old age at Edinburgh Zoo, where Polish veterans visited him.
- **Why it animates:** a cub in a soldier's cap, a bear in the back of a truck, a bear marching with a crate, the badge stamping onto a jacket.
- **Fact status:** documented (adoption, enlistment, badge, zoo). Carrying shells is reported by veterans, so it's labelled anecdote. Keep the war offscreen; no fighting shown.

## 6. The Man Who Crowned Himself Emperor of America
- **Hook:** "In 1859, a broke man in San Francisco declared himself Emperor of the United States, and the city played along."
- **Story:** Joshua Norton loses his fortune and sends a proclamation to a newspaper, which prints it. He wears a uniform, inspects the streets and issues his own money.
- **Ending (engagement):** Restaurants accepted his banknotes. When he died in 1880, around ten thousand people lined the streets. People still campaign to name the Bay Bridge after him.
- **Why it animates:** a newspaper headline, a plumed hat and epaulettes, shopkeepers bowing, a crowd filling the street.
- **Fact status:** documented (the newspaper proclamation, the banknotes, the 1880 funeral; sources give 10,000 to 30,000 people).

## 7. The Girl Who Found a Sea Monster
- **Hook:** "A girl who sold seashells on the beach found a sea monster, and the scientists took the credit."
- **Story:** Around 1811 in Lyme Regis, Mary Anning and her brother dig a 5-metre ichthyosaur out of the cliffs. Mary goes on to find the first complete plesiosaur.
- **Ending (moral):** Men published her finds and the Geological Society wouldn't let her join, yet today she's called the greatest fossil hunter ever known.
- **Why it animates:** a stormy cliff, a skeleton emerging from the rock piece by piece, gentlemen in top hats taking the fossil, her name finally carved in stone.
- **Fact status:** documented. The "she sells seashells" tongue twister link is unproven, so the script only nods to it.

## 8. The Clerk Whose Letter Shocked Cambridge
- **Hook:** "A poor office clerk in India mailed his maths to Cambridge, and the professor thought it was a prank."
- **Story:** In January 1913, Srinivasa Ramanujan sends G. H. Hardy pages of formulas without proofs. Hardy decides they must be true "because no one would have the imagination to invent them", and brings him to England.
- **Ending (engagement):** Hardy once said his taxi number, 1729, was boring. Ramanujan answered instantly that it's the smallest number you can make from two cubes in two different ways.
- **Why it animates:** formulas flying off the page like fireworks, a letter crossing oceans, a taxi with 1729 on it, cubes stacking up.
- **Fact status:** documented. Ramanujan said the goddess Namagiri gave him formulas in dreams; that's his own account. Hardy's quote should be checked before use.

## 9. The Real Robinson Crusoe
- **Hook:** "A sailor asked to be left on a desert island, and changed his mind as the ship sailed away."
- **Story:** In 1704, Alexander Selkirk argues that his ship is unsafe and asks to be put ashore in the Juan Fernández Islands. He survives four years and four months alone, on lobsters and wild goats.
- **Ending (twist):** His old ship really did sink. Ten years after his rescue, a novel called *Robinson Crusoe* appeared.
- **Why it animates:** the ship leaving, a sailor waving "wait!", a tally of scratches on a tree, goat-skin clothes, the rescue sails.
- **Fact status:** documented (Woodes Rogers' voyage account). The goats and the sunken ship need a quick check before scripting.

## 10. The Teacher Who Went Over Niagara Falls
- **Hook:** "The first person to go over Niagara Falls in a barrel was a 63-year-old schoolteacher, on her birthday."
- **Story:** In 1901, Annie Edson Taylor is broke and needs money for her old age. She climbs into a padded pickle barrel and goes over Horseshoe Falls, 51 metres down.
- **Ending (moral):** She survived with just a cut on her head, and said nobody should ever try it again. Her manager ran off with the barrel, and she never got rich. Fame isn't the same as fortune.
- **Why it animates:** a barrel bobbing towards the edge, the long drop in slow motion, the lid popping open, a dizzy but smiling teacher.
- **Fact status:** documented (newspapers of 1901, Guinness World Records). The cat that supposedly went first in a test run is an anecdote.
