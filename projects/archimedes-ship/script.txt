Archimedes once claimed he could move the whole Earth. So the king said: prove it.
The story goes, it happened in Syracuse, over two thousand years ago. Archimedes wrote to King Hiero: with the right machine, any weight can be moved by a small force. Give me another world to stand on, and I'll move this one.
The king had a better test. He picked one of the biggest ships in his navy, with three masts, dragged up onto the beach by crowds of men. Then he filled it with cargo and passengers.
Dozens of sailors couldn't budge it. And Archimedes was just one man.
So he didn't use muscle. He used rope.
He ran one rope around a set of wheels called pulleys, back and forth, again and again. Here's the trick. Every loop of rope shares the weight. With ten loops, each one holds only a tenth. He has to pull ten times more rope, but with a tenth of the strength.
Then Archimedes sat down, far away, and gently pulled.
And the giant ship slid across the sand toward him, as smoothly as if it were floating.
Today, every crane, elevator and tow truck uses that same idea.
Trade distance for strength, and one person can move a ship.
