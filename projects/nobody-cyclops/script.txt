The smartest trick in Greek myth was a fake name.
In Homer's ancient poem, Odysseus is sailing home from the Trojan War. He and twelve men wander into a giant cave full of cheese and sheep.
Then the owner comes home. Polyphemus, a Cyclops. A one-eyed giant as tall as a house. He rolls a boulder across the door, a rock so heavy that twenty-two wagons couldn't move it. And he decides the sailors are dinner.
They can't fight him. Even if they won, nobody else could move that rock.
So Odysseus pours the giant strong wine. And when the giant asks his name, Odysseus smiles. "My name is Nobody."
That night, while the giant sleeps, they strike his one eye with a burning stake. He screams for help. The other Cyclopes shout, "Who's hurting you?"
"Nobody is hurting me!"
So everyone goes back to bed.
In the morning, the blind giant rolls the stone aside and feels each sheep's back as it walks out. But the men are clinging underneath their bellies.
A perfect plan. Until, safe on his ship, Odysseus can't resist. He shouts his real name.
The giant's father is Poseidon, god of the sea. His curse keeps Odysseus from home for ten years.
One proud sentence ruined a perfect plan.
