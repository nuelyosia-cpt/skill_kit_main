How do you bring down a giant you can't even fight?
In Homer's ancient poem, Odysseus and twelve men are trapped in a cave with Polyphemus, a one-eyed giant as tall as a house. The door is sealed with a boulder twenty-two wagons couldn't move. And the sailors are dinner.
Here's the problem. Even if they beat the giant, nobody else could move that rock. They need him alive, to open the door for them.
So Odysseus makes a plan.
He sharpens a wooden stake and hides it. Then he pours the giant strong wine until he's sleepy.
When the giant asks his name, Odysseus is ready. "My name is Nobody."
That night, they strike the giant's one eye with the stake. He screams for help. His neighbours shout, "Who's hurting you?"
"Nobody is hurting me!"
So everyone goes back to bed. A cry for help that says nothing.
At dawn, the blind giant rolls the stone aside and feels every sheep that walks out. But the men are clinging underneath their bellies.
The giant opens the door for them himself.
Brains beat size.
Although, as we all know, he would still make a mistake he'd regret in the end.
