The Post-it Note was made from a glue that failed.
It's nineteen sixty-eight. At 3M, a chemist named Spencer Silver is trying to make a super-strong glue.
Instead, he gets the opposite. A glue so weak it barely holds paper.
You can peel it off and stick it back. Again and again.
But a glue that doesn't really glue? Nobody wants it.
For years, Silver shows it to anyone who'll listen. Nobody finds a good use for it.
Then, at church choir practice, a 3M engineer named Art Fry has a problem.
His paper bookmarks keep falling out of his hymn book, mid-song.
And he remembers Silver's useless glue.
A bookmark that sticks, then peels off without tearing the page.
Even then, the first test launch flops. People just don't get it.
So 3M hands out free pads in one city, Boise, Idaho.
Almost everyone who tries them wants more.
In nineteen eighty, Post-it Notes go on sale. Today, they're stuck on desks all over the world.
Sometimes a failure is just an idea that hasn't found its problem yet.
