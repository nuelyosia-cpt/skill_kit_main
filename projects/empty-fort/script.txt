The most famous bluff in Chinese history was a wide-open gate.
It's the year two twenty-eight, and China is split into three kingdoms at war.
Zhuge Liang, the genius strategist of Shu, has a terrible problem.
His general Ma Su ignored orders and lost a mountain pass.
Now, the story goes, a hundred and fifty thousand enemy soldiers are marching on the tiny town of Xicheng.
Leading them is his greatest rival, Sima Yi.
And inside the walls? A few thousand men. Mostly cooks, clerks, and very nervous horses.
He can't fight. He can't run. So he does the strangest thing imaginable.
He throws the gates open.
Soldiers dressed as townsfolk sweep the road.
And Zhuge Liang sits on top of the wall and plays the zither.
Sima Yi rides up and stares. Zhuge Liang never takes risks.
An open gate means a trap. It has to be.
So he turns his army around and leaves.
One man and a song beat a hundred and fifty thousand.
Sometimes the best defense is letting your enemy scare himself.
